Two clocks. One timeline.

Minecraft seeds its HUD’s random generator from a tick counter. The hunger bar jolts at zero saturation, and low-health hearts shake every tick. Matching a series of these patterns can recover the game tick.

Review the gaps

A forward jump can indicate footage removed, footage sped up, or another game instance. Lag and pauses make the game clock fall behind.

What a result can tell you

A result is evidence to review, not an automatic cheating verdict. No matched ticks means the scan is inconclusive. Unreadable HUDs, saturation, modified textures, transitions, and gaps can hide changes. Review the intervals and source frames.

Local by design

Videos are read directly from the file you select. They are never sent to GitHub or a processing server. The app keeps temporary working data, and refreshing or clearing the session discards it. Download a report if you want to keep the results.

Browser differences

The browser version uses the original pixel detector, RNG matching, and timeline analysis, with a new video reader and interface. It scans every frame. Native GPU controls, yt-dlp downloads, and executable updates are desktop features. Browser pixel conversion can differ from FFmpeg, so difficult footage may produce different readings.
